Security Audit Report — DcntEth (Decent Bridge)

Target: DcntEth (ERC20 + LayerZero OFTV2 bridge token) + Roles (AccessControl wrapper), built on LayerZero's standard OFTV2/LzApp stack and OpenZeppelin AccessControl/Ownable/ERC20.
Deployed instance audited: 0x1572D48a52906B834FB236AA77831d669F6d87A1 (Base), verified source pulled from Sourcify.
Client focus (as specified): (1) access control risks around mint/burn (router vs admin), (2) centralization or misconfiguration risks, (3) bridge-related or pause-related issues that could impact token safety.
Methodology: Two-phase audit — Phase 1 breadth (6 domain-checklist agents: general, precision-math, access-control, erc20, bridges, assembly) → Phase 2 depth (12 independent attacker-mindset agents, run blind to Phase 1) → hybrid reconciliation (this document).
Scope note: The verified source is a Foundry-layout repo (27 files) containing the two CUSTOM contracts that are the actual audit target — DcntEth.sol (~44 lines) and Roles.sol (~14 lines) — plus LayerZero's widely-deployed standard V1 OFTV2/BaseOFTV2/OFTCoreV2/LzApp/NonblockingLzApp implementation and OpenZeppelin's AccessControl/Ownable/ERC20. Because the client explicitly asked for bridge-related review, the LayerZero stack was read in full and scrutinized for how the custom contracts configure and extend it — not re-audited as novel code, but not skipped either. The out-of-scope DecentEthRouter contract (the primary caller of mint/burn, referenced throughout but not in this source bundle) was not available for this audit; several findings' full severity depends on its logic and are noted as such.

Reconciliation Summary

  • Total agents run: 18 (6 Phase-1 breadth + 12 Phase-2 depth).
  • Strongest corroboration of the audit: the sharedDecimals == 18 transfer-cap issue was independently discovered by 9 of 12 Phase-2 agents plus flagged as a lead by 3 of 6 Phase-1 agents (precision-math, erc20, bridges) — unanimous convergence from every angle (math-precision, execution-trace, invariant, periphery, first-principles, asymmetry, boundary, numerical-gap, flow-gap).
  • Overlap: every Medium+ finding below was found independently by both phases; several (admin mint/burn centralization, split-privilege-systems, missing pause) were each rediscovered by 8-11 of the 18 total agents.
  • Phase-2-only additions: two narrower leads not raised in Phase 1 — permissionless sendFrom bypassing router accounting, and an allowance-check asymmetry between the router's burn() and OFTV2's internal _debitFrom.
  • No Critical or High severity findings confirmable from the in-scope source alone. The most severe risks (admin/router unbacked minting draining real ETH backing) are real, concrete, privilege-gated mechanisms — but their end-to-end fund-loss impact depends on the out-of-scope DecentEthRouter's redemption logic, which no agent could read. They are reported at Medium with this dependency explicitly flagged; if the router blindly honors DcntEth balances 1:1 for redemption (plausible, unverified), these should be treated as High.

Findings

[1] sharedDecimals == 18 caps every single cross-chain transfer at ~18.4467 ETH

Severity: Medium
Origin: [both] — Phase 1: Precision-1/Bridges-3 (found by 3 of 6 agents). Phase 2: found independently by 9 of 12 agents — the single most corroborated finding of this audit.
Location: DcntEth constructor (OFTV2("Decent Eth", "DcntEth", 18, _layerZeroEndpoint)), OFTV2.ld2sdRate computation, OFTCoreV2._ld2sd()
Description: DcntEth's constructor passes _sharedDecimals = 18, equal to the token's own decimals() = 18. This sets ld2sdRate = 10**(decimals - sharedDecimals) = 10**0 = 1. LayerZero's OFTV2 wire format packs the cross-chain transfer amount into a uint64 field (amountSD = amount / ld2sdRate); with ld2sdRate = 1, amountSD equals the raw wei amount, so _ld2sd's guard require(amountSD <= type(uint64).max) directly caps every sendFrom/sendAndCall at type(uint64).max wei ≈ 18.4467 ETH. Standard OFTV2 deployments use sharedDecimals = 6-8 precisely to give this field enough headroom for realistic amounts (accepting minor dust truncation in exchange). Since sharedDecimals/ld2sdRate are immutable, this is fixed for the life of the deployed contract.
Proof of Concept: sendFrom(from, dstChainId, to, 20 ether, callParams) → _send → _ld2sd(20e18) computes 20e18 > 18,446,744,073,709,551,615 → reverts "OFTCore: amountSD overflow". The burn is rolled back atomically — no fund loss — but any single cross-chain transfer of ≥ ~18.45 ETH is permanently impossible on this deployment. Large holders (or the router itself, rebalancing pooled liquidity) must fragment transfers into many sub-18.44-ETH transactions, each paying a full LayerZero message fee.
Additional risk noted by 3 agents: if any peer-chain deployment of DcntEth uses a different sharedDecimals than 18 (plausible if a future chain uses the conventional default), the encode/decode scale mismatches and the receiving chain would mint a wildly wrong amount (off by the ratio of the two rates). This must be verified as identical across every deployed chain — not confirmable from this source bundle alone.
Recommendation: Redeploy with a conventional sharedDecimals (e.g. 8), identical across every chain in the deployment mesh, so ld2sdRate = 1e10 and the cap rises to ~1.8e11 ETH. If sharedDecimals = 18 (exact-wei fidelity, zero dust) is intentional, document the ~18.4467 ETH per-transfer ceiling prominently for integrators and front-ends so they chunk larger amounts automatically.

[2] Admin (mintByAdmin/burnByAdmin) and router (mint/burn) hold uncapped, unpausable mint/burn over the entire supply

Severity: Medium (would be High if the out-of-scope router redeems DcntEth 1:1 with no independent backing check — unverified)
Origin: [both] — Phase 1: AccessControl-1, General-2, ERC20-2, Bridges-1 (found by 4 of 6 agents). Phase 2: corroborated by 11 of 12 agents.
Location: DcntEth.mint()/burn() (lines 25-31, onlyRouter), mintByAdmin()/burnByAdmin() (lines 33-39, onlyAdmin), setRouter() (lines 21-23, onlyAdmin)
Description: DcntEth represents ETH bridged/locked via the router, with an implicit 1:1 backing invariant. Four entry points can break that invariant with no cap, rate limit, or timelock: mint/burn (gated by msg.sender == router, itself admin-settable via setRouter) and mintByAdmin/burnByAdmin (gated by DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE). Both burn and burnByAdmin call OZ _burn directly with no allowance check, so the router/admin can destroy any holder's balance — an EOA, a DEX pool, a lending vault — with no consent. There is no pause mechanism anywhere (see finding [4]).
Proof of Concept: Admin (or a compromised/malicious router, itself admin-repointable via setRouter) calls mintByAdmin(attacker, 1_000_000e18) — succeeds, no cap, no ETH locked anywhere. Attacker bridges/redeems through the router for real locked ETH, or dumps directly into a DEX pool paired against DcntEth, draining real value from honest holders/LPs. Separately, burnByAdmin(victimPool, victimBalance) can zero out a DEX pool's DcntEth reserve or any user's balance unilaterally.
Dependency: Full severity (Medium vs. High) hinges on the out-of-scope DecentEthRouter's redemption logic — not available for this audit. Recommend auditing it separately to close this gap.
Recommendation: Remove mintByAdmin/burnByAdmin or gate behind a timelock + multisig with a hard cap. Add Pausable (guarded by a fast-response guardian role distinct from full admin) on mint/burn and the cross-chain receive path. Require allowance for burn(from, …) unless from == msg.sender. Add a per-tx/rolling-window mint rate limit.

[3] Two independent, unsynchronized privilege systems (Ownable.owner() vs DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE) double the god-mode attack surface

Severity: Medium
Origin: [both] — Phase 1: AccessControl-2, General-3 (found by 2 of 6 agents). Phase 2: corroborated by 8 of 12 agents.
Location: DcntEth dual inheritance OFTV2, Roles (line 6); Ownable auto-sets owner = msg.sender at construction; Roles(msg.sender) grants DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE to the same deployer
Description: DcntEth has two fully independent, mutually-unaware authority systems, both defaulting to the deployer at construction so any divergence is invisible at deploy time: (1) Ownable.owner() (via OFTV2 → ... → LzApp) controls ALL LayerZero trust configuration — setTrustedRemote(Address), setConfig, setSend/ReceiveVersion, forceResumeReceive, setMinDstGas, setPayloadSizeLimit, setPrecrime; (2) AccessControl.DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE (via Roles) controls setRouter, mintByAdmin, burnByAdmin, and grantRole. Critically, owner-level power is also a minting oracle: owner can call setTrustedRemoteAddress to trust an attacker-controlled remote, after which a crafted cross-chain message passes the lzReceive trusted-remote check and mints arbitrary DcntEth via _creditTo. So the owner key is just as capable of unbacked minting as the DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE key — through a different door. If the team migrates one system to a multisig/timelock and forgets the other, the stale deployer key retains full god-mode over its half.
Proof of Concept: Team calls transferOwnership(multisig), believing the contract is now multisig-controlled. DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE is still held by the original hot deployer key (never migrated via grantRole/revokeRole). Attacker who compromises that key calls mintByAdmin(...) and setRouter(...) — full control despite the "handover." (Symmetric: admin migrated but owner forgotten → attacker uses setTrustedRemoteAddress + a forged inbound message to mint via _creditTo.)
Recommendation: Collapse to a single authority model (route the Ownable LZ setters through the same DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE check, or vice versa), or add an explicit deployment-time assertion/runbook that keeps both pointed at the same governance address. Document both systems prominently for any handover.

[4] No pause/circuit-breaker anywhere in the stack — and the closest thing to a kill switch doesn't actually work

Severity: Low
Origin: [both] — Phase 1: General-1, Bridges-1 (explicit client ask, confirmed via exhaustive grep across the entire source tree — zero hits for pause-related patterns). Phase 2: corroborated by 9 of 12 agents.
Location: whole contract (no Pausable anywhere in DcntEth, Roles, or the LayerZero stack)
Description: Directly answers the client's explicit question #3: there is no pause/circuit-breaker mechanism anywhere. If the router key, an admin key, or the LayerZero trust configuration is ever compromised, there is no on-chain emergency stop for minting. New nuance from Phase 2's flow-gap agent: even the closest thing to an admin "kill switch," setRouter(address(0)), only disables the mint/burn router path — it does not stop sendFrom-driven burns or inbound lzReceive → _creditTo → _mint cross-chain bridging, which remain live as long as any trustedRemote is configured (a separate, Ownable-gated system). So an admin responding to an active incident cannot actually halt cross-chain minting via any single action available in this contract.
Recommendation: Add Pausable (guarded by a fast-response guardian role distinct from the full admin) on mint, burn, mintByAdmin, burnByAdmin, and ideally the _creditTo/_debitFrom receive/send paths, so an incident can be contained with one transaction rather than requiring coordinated setRouter + setTrustedRemote changes across two separate privilege systems.

[5] setRouter lacks zero-address check and event emission

Severity: Low
Origin: [both] — Phase 1: AccessControl-4 (found by 2 of 6 agents). Phase 2: corroborated by 6 of 12 agents.
Location: setRouter() (lines 21-23)
Description: No zero-address validation and no event emission. setRouter(0) silently disables the router mint/burn path (bridge liveness DoS until corrected). Absence of an event blinds off-chain monitoring to changes of this security-critical address, which the router — once set — holds unlimited mint authority through.
Recommendation: Add require(_router != address(0)) and emit event RouterSet(address indexed oldRouter, address indexed newRouter).

[6] Single-step ownership transfer and exposed renounceOwnership can permanently brick LayerZero configuration

Severity: Low
Origin: [phase 1 only] — AccessControl-3. Re-examined: confirmed against source; corroborated as a general "split-privilege" symptom by several Phase 2 agents but not raised as its own distinct item there.
Location: inherited OZ Ownable (single-step transferOwnership), renounceOwnership() reachable
Description: The LZ side uses standard (not 2-step) Ownable. A typo'd transferOwnership target permanently loses control of all cross-chain trust config (setTrustedRemote, forceResumeReceive, etc.). renounceOwnership() is also reachable and, once called, permanently prevents any future trusted-remote changes or unblocking a stuck LayerZero message queue — a latent permanent DoS on the messaging path.
Recommendation: Use Ownable2Step; consider overriding renounceOwnership to revert given the contract requires a live owner for cross-chain liveness.

[7] Unrestricted DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE proliferation

Severity: Low
Origin: [both] — Phase 1: AccessControl-5. Phase 2: corroborated as part of the general split-privilege leads.
Location: inherited AccessControl.grantRole, not overridden
Description: DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE is its own role-admin; any admin can grant DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE to unlimited arbitrary addresses with no cap or timelock. A compromised admin can immediately clone itself, making revocation a losing race.
Recommendation: Route admin-role changes through a timelock, or cap membership.

[8] Permissionless sendFrom bypasses the router's own accounting

Severity: Info
Origin: [phase 2 only] — first-principles agent.
Location: BaseOFTV2.sendFrom() (public, inherited, unmodified)
Description: If any non-router address ever holds DcntEth (e.g. via mintByAdmin, or if a future router design mints directly to users), that holder can call the public, permissionless sendFrom to burn-and-rebridge outside the router's tracked backing — potentially desyncing the router's per-chain WETH accounting. Unverifiable without the out-of-scope router source; flagged for the team to confirm the router's accounting model tolerates this.
Recommendation: Confirm with the router design whether user-held DcntEth bridging via sendFrom (bypassing router mint/burn) is accounted for; if not, document the risk or restrict transfers.

[9] burn() (router path) has no allowance check, unlike OFTV2's own internal _debitFrom

Severity: Info
Origin: [phase 2 only] — trust-gap agent.
Location: DcntEth.burn() (line 29) vs. OFTV2._debitFrom()
Description: DcntEth.burn(_from, _amount) (onlyRouter) calls _burn directly with no _spendAllowance check, while the OFT bridge's own internal burn path (OFTV2._debitFrom) does check allowance when _from != spender. Same economic effect (destroy a user's balance) reached under two different trust assumptions — only exploitable by the (trusted, out-of-scope) router itself, so this is a consistency/trust-surface note rather than a standalone vulnerability.
Recommendation: Confirm the router only ever burns from balances it has independently authenticated (e.g. its own escrow, or a user action it directly observed) rather than arbitrary third-party balances.

[10] Floating pragma ^0.8.13

Severity: Info
Origin: [phase 1 only] — General-5.
Location: DcntEth.sol:2, Roles.sol:2
Description: Allows compilation with any 0.8.x ≥ 0.8.13, including ≥0.8.20 (PUSH0-emitting). Base supports PUSH0 (not an active break), but the deployed bytecode/compiler is not pinned to what was actually reviewed.
Recommendation: Pin an exact compiler version matching what was tested/deployed.

Confirmed Non-Issues (all agents' explicit clears)

  • Standard public ERC20 surface (transfer/approve/transferFrom/decimals) is unmodified OpenZeppelin — none of the classic weird-ERC20 checklist items (fee-on-transfer, rebasing, blocklists, approve races, ERC777 hooks) apply; _beforeTokenTransfer is an empty no-op.
  • Replay/double-credit protection: NonblockingLzApp.retryMessage and creditedPackets in _sendAndCallAck correctly prevent replay/double-crediting on message retry, verified independently by multiple agents.
  • Payload decoding: _decodeSendPayload/_decodeSendAndCallPayload byte-offset parsing via BytesLib is correctly bounds-checked at every accessor (toAddress/toUint64/toBytes32/slice); no OOB read possible even without a top-level length check on _decodeSendAndCallPayload.
  • Assembly correctness: LzApp._getGasLimit's raw mload offset (34) is arithmetically correct for the documented adapterParams V1 format; ExcessivelySafeCall's 150-byte returndata truncation only affects the informational revert reason, never success/failure classification; no free-memory-pointer corruption or insufficient allocation anywhere in the assembly surface.
  • Dust-removal math: _removeDust/_ld2sd/_sd2ld is exact and safe given ld2sdRate = 1 (structurally neutralized — the flip side of finding [1]'s transfer cap is that there is zero dust loss).
  • forceResumeReceive: a pure liveness lever (skips a stuck message) — cannot be misused to mint or double-credit.
  • onlyRouter/onlyAdmin gating itself: correctly implemented (msg.sender == router, hasRole(DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE, msg.sender)); no privilege-escalation path exists for an unprivileged caller; supportsInterface correctly ORs both parent interfaces.
  • No proxy/upgradeability: constructor-only initialization, no initialize() pattern, ruling out init-hijack or storage-collision concerns.

Open Items (flagged for follow-up, not verifiable from on-chain source alone)

  • DecentEthRouter redemption logic (out of scope, not in this source bundle) — the full severity of findings [2] and [8] depends on whether the router blindly trusts DcntEth balances 1:1 for redemption. Recommend auditing the router contract separately; if confirmed, finding [2] should be escalated to High.
  • LayerZero V1 endpoint trust configuration — whether this deployment uses LayerZero's default single Oracle+Relayer (collusion = forged messages = unlimited mint) or a hardened custom DVN/oracle/relayer setup is set via owner transactions against the live endpoint, not visible in this source bundle. Recommend verifying on-chain config per deployed chain.
  • precrime monitor unset, minDstGas/trustedRemote lane values not determinable from source — recommend verifying these are correctly configured per deployment, especially that trustedRemoteLookup on each chain points only at the legitimate peer DcntEth.
  • Cross-chain sharedDecimals consistency — verify every deployed chain's DcntEth uses the same sharedDecimals (18, per this deployment) to avoid the scale-mismatch risk noted under finding [1].

Overall assessment: No unprivileged, no-precondition path to fund loss was found by either phase's agents across 18 independent runs. The confirmed issues cluster around three themes, all directly matching the client's stated focus: (1) a hard, deployment-configuration-driven transfer cap (~18.44 ETH) from an unconventional sharedDecimals choice — the most heavily corroborated finding of the audit; (2) centralization — admin and router both hold uncapped, unpausable mint/burn power with no on-chain constraint tying supply to real backing; (3) two independent, unsynchronized privilege systems (Ownable vs AccessControl) that can silently diverge during any partial handover, and no working emergency stop for cross-chain minting. We recommend the team prioritize fixing the sharedDecimals deployment parameter, adding a genuine circuit breaker across both the router and OFT bridging paths, and unifying or explicitly documenting the dual privilege-system before this contract handles significant value. We further recommend the client commission a follow-up audit of the DecentEthRouter contract, since several findings here (particularly [2] and [8]) cannot be fully resolved without it.
