Security Audit Report: Vaultfire AIAccountabilityBondsV2

Target: AIAccountabilityBondsV2 at 0x6750D28865434344e04e1D0a6044394b726C3dfE (Base, chain 8453)
In-scope contracts: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.sol, BaseYieldPoolBond.sol, BaseDignityBond.sol, MissionEnforcement.sol (source verified via Sourcify full match; compiler 0.8.25)
Methodology: Two-phase audit — Phase 1 breadth (6 parallel checklist-driven agents: general, precision-math, access-control, oracles, dos, governance) → Phase 2 depth (12 parallel attacker-mindset agents, run blind to Phase 1) → hybrid reconciliation (this document).
Reconciliation summary: Overlap: 10 findings independently confirmed by both phases · Phase-1-only: 8 · Phase-2-only: 2 (both re-examined against source and confirmed) · Demoted on re-examine: 0.
Completeness: every unique (contract, function, bug-class) raised by either phase's raw agent output is represented below (directly, or folded into a closely related finding with its distinct mechanism preserved).

Summary

This protocol lets an AI company post an ETH "accountability bond," self-report six "human flourishing" metrics about itself, and — after a 7-day timelock — claim up to 50% of the bond's self-computed "appreciation" from a communally funded yield pool. Both audit phases converged, independently, on the same central defect: the party that profits from the metrics is the only party that supplies them, and none of the contract's advertised counterbalances (an oracle registry, a minimum-peer-verification threshold, a community challenge mechanism, a 7-day "verification window") actually gates the payout. On top of that architectural flaw, Phase 2 discovered a cheap, unauthenticated griefing vector that lets any address permanently zero out a bond's AI profit share for the cost of a few wei, and several accounting bugs let owner-level or ordinary operations desynchronize the yield pool's internal ledger from the real ETH backing it.

High Severity

[H-1] Self-attested flourishing metrics let any bond drain the community-funded yield pool

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (general, precision-math, oracles) · Phase 2 (8/12 agents: math-precision, economic-security, execution-trace, invariant, periphery, first-principles, boundary, trust-gap)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.submitMetrics (447-476), calculateBondValue (978-993), distributeBond (758-873), shouldLockProfits (1062-1092)

submitMetrics is gated only by onlyAICompany(bondId) — the bond's own beneficiary supplies all six 0-10000 "flourishing" scores that directly drive calculateBondValue, and thus the appreciation paid out of the shared yieldPool. The contract declares an oracle registry (OracleSource, registerOracle, MIN_ORACLE_TRUST_SCORE=7000) and a peer-verification threshold (MIN_AI_VERIFICATIONS=2), but neither is ever read on the metrics, valuation, or distribution path (confirmed by grep across every agent in both phases: oracles[...] is read exactly once, inside registerOracle itself, solely to dedupe a push). shouldLockProfits's verification gate defaults to a passing score of 5000 when zero verifications exist.

Proof of concept (numbers independently reproduced by 4+ agents):

createBond("x", 0) with msg.value = 10 ETH        // quarterlyRevenue self-declared as 0, trivially satisfies the 30% check
submitMetrics(id, 10000,10000,10000,10000,10000,10000)   // self-reported by the caller

globalFlourishingScore = 10000
inclusionMultiplier    = 150 + (10000-7000)/60 = 200
timeMultiplier (fresh) = 100
calculateBondValue     = 10e18 * 10000 * 200 * 100 / 50_000_000 = 40 ETH
calculateAppreciation  = 40 - 10 = 30 ETH

shouldLockProfits: flourishing 10000 ≥ 4000 (not suffering); one metric row ⇒ no declining trend;
                   inclusion 10000 ≥ 4000; verificationQualityScore = 5000 (zero verifications) ≥ 3000
                   ⇒ NOT locked

requestDistribution → snapshots +30 ETH (needs yieldPool ≥ 30 ETH)
[wait 7 days]
distributeBond → _useYieldPool(30 ETH) drains the community yieldPool;
                 humanShare = 15 ETH, aiCompanyShare = 15 ETH

Net: attacker staked 10 ETH, received 15 ETH → +5 ETH profit, funded entirely
by other users' yieldPool contributions. Repeatable per bond, scales further
with bond age via timeMultiplier (see L-1).

Recommendation: Require verificationConfirmCount[bondId] >= MIN_AI_VERIFICATIONS and/or an active, trust-scored oracle attestation as a hard precondition in requestDistribution/shouldLockProfits; treat the zero-verification case as a lock condition rather than a passing default.

[H-2] The profit-lock snapshot at requestDistribution makes the 7-day community-verification window functionally inert

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (oracles agent, Finding 3) · Phase 2 (economic-security, trust-gap, flow-gap — 3/12 agents independently)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.requestDistribution (706-733), distributeBond (758-873)

snapshotProfitsLocked[bondId] = shouldLockProfits(bondId) is captured once, at requestDistribution time, and distributeBond reads only that frozen value at line 786 — it never re-evaluates shouldLockProfits live. Because the AI company chooses when to call requestDistribution (and can call submitMetrics immediately beforehand), it can always snapshot the favorable "not locked" state before any community challengeMetrics or adverse peer verification lands. The snapshot is asymmetric by construction: it can only ever freeze the AI-favorable outcome, since an AI facing a live lock simply defers requesting.

Proof: Two challengeMetrics calls during the 7-day window drop the live verificationQualityScore to 5000 - 2*2000 = 1000 < 3000 (which would trigger a lock), but distributeBond still reads the pre-challenge snapshotProfitsLocked=false recorded before the challenges existed, and pays the normal 50/50 split regardless. The contract's own NatSpec states the 7-day delay exists so "humans need time to verify if flourishing claims are accurate" (line 770) and "Timelock prevents instant rug pull" (line 704) — neither claim holds for the lock decision itself.

Recommendation: Re-evaluate shouldLockProfits (at minimum its challenge/verification component) live at distributeBond time, and lock if either the snapshot or the live evaluation says to lock.

[H-3] submitAIVerification rejections are free, unbounded, and irreversible — any address can permanently zero a bond's AI profit share for ~1–2 wei

Origin: [phase2 only] — confirmed via source re-read; found independently as a FINDING by 3/12 agents (access-control, execution-trace, periphery) plus a related sybil-abuse variant by a 4th (economic-security)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.submitAIVerification (548-578), verificationQualityScore (1020-1050), shouldLockProfits (1062-1092)

function submitAIVerification(uint256 bondId, bool confirmsMetrics, string memory notes)
    external payable bondExists(bondId) whenNotPaused
{
    Bond storage bond = bonds[bondId];
    require(msg.sender != bond.aiCompany, "Cannot verify own metrics");
    _validateNonZero(msg.value, "Verification stake");     // 1 wei satisfies this
    require(bytes(notes).length > 0 && bytes(notes).length <= 500, "Notes invalid");
    ...
    if (confirmsMetrics) { verificationConfirmCount[bondId]++; }
    else { verificationRejectCount[bondId]++; }             // no per-address dedup, no cap
    ...
}

verificationQualityScore computes score = 5000 - int256(rejectCount * 3000), clamped to [0, 10000]. No function anywhere in scope decrements verificationRejectCount — unlike MetricsChallenge, which has an owner-adjudicated resolveChallenge path, a rejecting "verification" has no dispute or reversal mechanism at all.

Proof (re-verified directly against source): Any address (≠ bond.aiCompany) calls submitAIVerification(bondId, false, "x") twice (1 wei each, distinct or same wallet — no dedup check exists) → verificationRejectCount[bondId]=2 → verificationQualityScore = 5000 - 6000 = -1000, clamped to 0 → 0 < 3000 → shouldLockProfits returns (true, "Failed verification...") permanently (no path ever removes the rejections). Every future requestDistribution on that bond snapshots locked=true, and distributeBond sends 100% of appreciation to humanTreasury and 0% to the AI company — for the life of the bond. Total attacker cost: ~2 wei + gas.

Related sybil variant (same root cause, opposite direction): the identical lack of verifier identity/stake requirements lets the AI company itself defeat legitimate community challenges by self-confirming from throwaway addresses — 2 confirmations at 1 wei each add +2000 to verificationQualityScore, capable of canceling out up to 2 real community challenges (-4000).

Recommendation: Require a meaningful, slashable stake for both confirmations and rejections; add per-verifier/per-bond dedup; add an adjudication/dispute path (mirroring resolveChallenge) that can reverse bad-faith votes in either direction; cap the rejection penalty symmetrically with the confirmation bonus.

[H-4] resolveChallenge pays a 2x "upheld" penalty from commingled contract balance, not from the challenged bond's own stake

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (access-control Medium, governance HIGH-1, general Medium-1) · Phase 2 (6/12 agents as leads: access-control, invariant, asymmetry, boundary, trust-gap, flow-gap — promoted to finding per multi-agent corroboration + fully traced exploit chain)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.resolveChallenge (643-684)

if (upheld) {
    uint256 penalty = challenge.challengeStake;              // 1:1 penalty
    payoutAmount = challenge.challengeStake + penalty;        // 2x stake
    recipient = payable(challenge.challenger);
    require(address(this).balance >= payoutAmount, ...);
} ...
(bool success, ) = recipient.call{value: payoutAmount}("");

The extra 1x "penalty" is paid from address(this).balance — the same commingled pot backing every bond's stake, every AI-verification stake, and the yield pool — and is never debited from the challenged bond's own stakeAmount, totalActiveBondValue, or yieldPool, despite the function's own NatSpec claiming "Challenger gets stake back + penalty from bond" (line 638).

Proof: yieldPool=10 ETH (contract balance 10 ETH). Challenger stakes 5 ETH via challengeMetrics (balance 15 ETH). Owner resolves upheld=true: pays out 5+5=10 ETH (balance 5 ETH). The yieldPool accounting variable is unchanged at 10 — now exceeding the real unencumbered balance. A colluding owner + challenger pair can repeat this cycle to drain third-party (other bonds'/pool depositors') funds one stake-multiple at a time; even a fully honest resolution breaks pool solvency, causing later legitimate distributeBond calls to revert on require(address(this).balance >= totalPayout).

Recommendation: Source the penalty from the challenged bond's own stake (revert the resolution if the bond can't cover it) or from a dedicated slashing reserve funded specifically for this purpose — never draw penalties from general contract balance shared with other users' principal.

[H-5] AI-to-AI verification stakes are permanently unrecoverable — no refund, slash, or claim path exists

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (general, HIGH-2) · Phase 2 (5/12 agents: execution-trace, asymmetry, boundary, trust-gap, flow-gap)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.submitAIVerification (548-578), AIVerification struct (102-110)

AIVerification.stakeAmount = msg.value is written once at submission time and — confirmed independently by grep across nine separate agents — never read anywhere by any refund, slash, claim, or resolution logic in any of the four in-scope contracts. Every wei sent as a verification "stake on their verification" (the NatSpec's own "skin in the game" framing) is a one-way, irrecoverable transfer into the contract's general balance.

Proof: Any address calling submitAIVerification{value: v}(...) loses v permanently. There is no function in AIAccountabilityBondsV2, BaseYieldPoolBond, or BaseDignityBond that ever references AIVerification.stakeAmount after it is stored.

Recommendation: Add a refund/claim mechanism tied to verification outcome (e.g., release the stake once the associated bond distributes, or slash only via a provably-false-verification adjudication path), or remove the payable/staking requirement entirely if it was never intended to be economically meaningful.

[H-6] Bond principal is never returned — and a bond with zero appreciation can never be closed at all

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (precision-math lead, general Medium-3) · Phase 2 (6/12 agents; boundary agent proves the unclosable-neutral-bond variant)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.distributeBond (758-873), requestDistribution → _trackPendingDistribution (194-210)

distributeBond only ever transfers humanShare + aiCompanyShare = absAppreciation; the original bond.stakeAmount is never sent back to the AI company, and there is no other withdrawal function for it anywhere in scope — contradicting the NatSpec's framing of distributing "bond proceeds." This is compounded by a hard blocker: both _trackPendingDistribution and distributeBond require(appreciation != 0), and bond.active is only ever cleared inside distributeBond. A bond sitting at exactly the neutral valuation point can therefore never call requestDistribution successfully and can never be closed — permanently trapping its principal with zero recovery path.

Proof: A bond with no metrics submitted (or metrics averaging exactly to the breakeven point) has flourishing=5000 (default), inclusion=100, time=100 → calculateBondValue = stake * 5000 * 100 * 100 / 50_000_000 = stake exactly → appreciation = 0 → requestDistribution reverts with "No appreciation to distribute," permanently, for that bond. No withdraw/cancelBond/refund function exists in any in-scope contract.

Recommendation: Add a principal-withdrawal/bond-closure path that does not depend on non-zero appreciation. Clarify and document whether stake forfeiture on distribution is intended; if not, return stakeAmount alongside the appreciation split.

Medium Severity

[M-1] withdrawYieldPool ignores totalPendingDistributions — owner can withdraw funds already reserved for pending distributions

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (access-control, governance, general) · Phase 2 (7/12 agents — near-unanimous: access-control, economic-security, execution-trace, invariant, asymmetry, boundary, flow-gap)
Location: BaseYieldPoolBond.withdrawYieldPool (102-116), setMinimumYieldPoolBalance (123-131), _trackPendingDistribution (194-210)

_trackPendingDistribution reserves totalPendingDistributions against the pool at request time as its core solvency invariant (require(yieldPool >= totalPendingDistributions + amount)), but withdrawYieldPool only checks yieldPool - amount >= minimumYieldPoolBalance — never subtracting totalPendingDistributions. The owner can also freely lower minimumYieldPoolBalance first (floor: > 0), further widening the gap.

Proof: yieldPool=100 ETH, minimumYieldPoolBalance=10 ETH. Bond A requests distribution with appreciation 90 ETH → totalPendingDistributions=90 (passes 100 >= 0+90). Owner calls withdrawYieldPool(90): 90<=100 ✓, 100-90=10>=10 ✓ → sends 90 ETH to owner, yieldPool=10. When Bond A's 7-day timelock expires, distributeBond reverts at _useYieldPool's require(yieldPool>=90). Since distributionPending is already true, Bond A cannot re-request — it is stuck until someone re-funds the pool via the permissionless fundYieldPool.

Recommendation: require(yieldPool - amount >= minimumYieldPoolBalance + totalPendingDistributions); add a floor to setMinimumYieldPoolBalance tied to outstanding obligations.

[M-2] _useYieldPool's minimum-reserve check uses the pre-deduction balance, letting a single distribution breach the circuit breaker

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (precision-math) · Phase 2 (boundary agent, full numeric trace; numerical-gap agent, independent confirmation)
Location: BaseYieldPoolBond._useYieldPool (156-185)

if (yieldPoolEnforced) {
    require(yieldPool >= minimumYieldPoolBalance, "Yield pool below minimum - distributions paused");
}
yieldPool -= amount;

The check evaluates the balance before deduction. The sibling function withdrawYieldPool correctly uses the post-deduction form (yieldPool - amount >= minimumYieldPoolBalance) — _useYieldPool deviates from the codebase's own established pattern for the same invariant.

Proof: pool=15 ETH, minimum=10 ETH, snapshotted appreciation=14 ETH. Distribute-time check: pre-deduction 15>=10 ✓, then subtract → pool=1 ETH — 9 ETH below the floor the circuit breaker exists to enforce, and every subsequent distribution now reverts on yieldPool >= minimum until the pool is refunded.

Recommendation: require(yieldPool - amount >= minimumYieldPoolBalance, ...) (post-deduction), matching withdrawYieldPool.

[M-3] MINIMUM_RESERVE_RATIO (50%) is computed but never enforced as a hard gate

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (precision-math, general, governance) · Phase 2 (numerical-gap agent — scale-mismatch variant showing the denominator itself is also wrong)
Location: BaseYieldPoolBond.getReserveRatio (255-266), _useYieldPool, withdrawYieldPool

The contract documents a 50%-of-active-bond-value reserve floor as a circuit breaker, but no require anywhere actually checks the ratio — _useYieldPool/withdrawYieldPool only check the flat minimumYieldPoolBalance, and getReserveRatio's low-ratio branch is a dead comment ("this is a view function, so we can't emit events... UI/monitoring should check this"). Compounding the gap, totalActiveBondValue (the ratio's denominator) accumulates bond principal, not the appreciation obligations the pool actually owes — which can reach ~11x principal at maximum multipliers — so even a "healthy" reported ratio can understate true exposure by an order of magnitude.

Proof: totalActiveBondValue=1000 ETH, minimumYieldPoolBalance=10 ETH, yieldPool=11 ETH (true ratio ≈1.1%, far below the 50% floor). withdrawYieldPool(1 ether) still succeeds (11-1=10>=10). The advertised 50% guarantee never actually holds, and getProtocolHealth()'s reserveRatioOK flag has no enforcement teeth.

Recommendation: Enforce getReserveRatio() >= MINIMUM_RESERVE_RATIO inside _useYieldPool/withdrawYieldPool (gated by yieldPoolEnforced), and compute the ratio against outstanding appreciation obligations rather than principal — or remove the constant/claim if enforcement isn't intended.

[M-4] Pending-distribution reservations have no cancel/expiry — can lock out the shared pool's capacity

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (dos, governance) · Phase 2 (4/12 agents; economic-security agent gives a full numeric griefing trace)
Location: BaseYieldPoolBond._trackPendingDistribution (194-210), AIAccountabilityBondsV2.requestDistribution (706-733)

_trackPendingDistribution reserves pool capacity in totalPendingDistributions with no cancellation path except actually completing distributeBond. The reservation logic also treats appreciation and depreciation identically (amount = |appreciationValue|), even though the depreciation branch of distributeBond never draws from yieldPool at all — it pays from general balance via _clearPendingDistribution — so an ordinary depreciating bond needlessly locks pool capacity it will never consume.

Proof: Attacker stakes ~33.3 ETH into a bond, submits maximal metrics (appreciation ≈ 3x stake ≈ 100 ETH), calls requestDistribution to reserve an entire 100 ETH pool, then never calls distributeBond. Every other bond's requestDistribution now reverts on require(yieldPool >= totalPendingDistributions + amount) until the pool is topped up or the griefer eventually distributes.

Recommendation: Add a cancelDistribution() (AI-callable, and/or auto-expiring after a bounded window) that clears the reservation; only reserve pool capacity for the appreciation (pool-payable) branch, not depreciation.

[M-5] MissionEnforcement is a separate contract with an independent, un-synced owner — can permanently brick all distributions

Origin: [phase1 primary] (access-control HIGH, governance HIGH-2, general Medium-4) — a related, narrower mid-timelock variant independently confirmed in Phase 2 (see cross-reference below)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2._requireMissionCompliance (315-336), setMissionEnforcement/setMissionEnforcementEnabled (303-313); MissionEnforcement.certifyModuleMissionCompliant/adjudicateMissionViolation (226-259)

_requireMissionCompliance() — called from both requestDistribution and distributeBond — hard-reverts unless MissionEnforcement.isCompliantWithPrinciple(address(this), ...) returns true for three principles. That mapping defaults to false and can only be flipped by MissionEnforcement's own, independent owner (set at MissionEnforcement's own constructor — the main contract's setMissionEnforcement only stores a pointer; it never verifies or syncs ownership or certification state).

Two concrete traces:

  1. Misconfiguration: main owner calls setMissionEnforcementEnabled(true) before the main contract has been certified compliant by MissionEnforcement's owner → every requestDistribution/distributeBond reverts "Mission: profit caps," protocol-wide, with no self-service fix available to the main protocol's owner.
  2. Hostile/absent second owner: MissionEnforcement's owner (a different party) can, at any later time, call adjudicateMissionViolation to flip compliance back to false, freezing all distributions unilaterally — even after a correct initial setup.

Cross-reference (Phase 2, agent 12, flow-gap): a narrower but independently confirmed variant of the same mechanism — an AI successfully calls requestDistribution while compliant, reserving pool capacity, and then the MissionEnforcement owner revokes compliance mid-timelock; distributeBond now permanently reverts for that specific bond (no cancel path exists, and distributionPending blocks re-request), stranding that bond's reserved pool capacity indefinitely as a side effect.

Recommendation: Sync MissionEnforcement's owner to the main protocol's owner (or have the main contract own its MissionEnforcement instance directly), require self-certification before setMissionEnforcementEnabled(true) can be called, and add a cancelDistribution so a bricked pending request doesn't permanently consume shared pool capacity.

[M-6] resolveChallenge centralizes disputed-claim adjudication in a single owner with no timelock, evidence requirement, or appeal

Origin: [phase1 only] (governance Medium-1) — re-examined against source and confirmed
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.resolveChallenge (643-684)

The owner alone and instantly decides upheld with only a free-text bool parameter — no on-chain evidence requirement, no delay, no appeal mechanism. This directly contradicts the "community can challenge any claim" mission principle advertised in MissionEnforcement.MISSION_STATEMENT, and centralizes disproportionate power in a single address with no counterbalance (compounds H-4's fund-drain mechanism).

Recommendation: Add a timelock/appeal window before a challenge resolution becomes final, and require on-chain evidence hashes; consider multi-party or committee-based adjudication rather than a single EOA.

[M-7] MissionEnforcement's "immutable, cannot be changed by any authority" claims are false — its substantive checks are never wired into distribution logic

Origin: [phase1 only] (access-control, governance) — re-examined against source and confirmed
Location: MissionEnforcement.sol NatSpec (lines 12, 22-23, 46-51, 298-300) vs. missionCompliance mapping and distributeBond

The contract's own documentation states its principles are "IMMUTABLE... enforced at the smart contract level... cannot be changed by governance, upgrades, or any authority." In code, compliance is a plain owner-writable mapping flipped freely by certifyModuleMissionCompliant/adjudicateMissionViolation. Separately, the substantive numerical checks — verifyAIProfitCap(aiShare, totalAmount, capPercentage) and verifyHumanFinalSay — are pure helper functions that are never called anywhere in distributeBond or requestDistribution; the actual 50/50 split is a hardcoded (abs+1)/2 calculation with no reference to the "cap" verification function at all.

Recommendation: Either wire verifyAIProfitCap/verifyHumanFinalSay into distributeBond as real, enforced post-conditions, or remove the "immutable/cryptographically enforced" language to avoid presenting a false security guarantee to users and integrators.

[M-8] setMissionEnforcement/setMissionEnforcementEnabled take effect instantly with no timelock, unlike the treasury's 48h delay

Origin: [phase1 only] (access-control) — re-examined against source and confirmed
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.setMissionEnforcement/setMissionEnforcementEnabled (303-313)

Unlike proposeHumanTreasury/acceptHumanTreasury (48-hour timelock), these two functions take effect immediately with no validation of the new module and no delay. The owner can instantly swap in a module that always returns true (neutering the mission gate entirely) or one that bricks it (see M-5).

Recommendation: Apply the same timelock pattern already used for treasury changes.

[M-9] Push payments to humanTreasury/challenger/AI company can brick the whole distributeBond/resolveChallenge call on a reverting recipient

Origin: [phase1 only] (dos) — re-examined against source and confirmed
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.distributeBond (853-861), resolveChallenge (680-681)

All payouts are hard require(success) pushes. If humanTreasury (owner-set) is, or later becomes, a contract that reverts on receive, every bond's distributeBond reverts — a protocol-wide freeze. It is recoverable only by the owner swapping treasuries via the existing 48-hour timelock, keeping this at Medium rather than High.

Recommendation: Convert at least the treasury and challenger legs to a pull-payment/claimable-balance pattern so a single bad recipient can't block others.

Low Severity

[L-1] timeMultiplier is unbounded, exceeding the documented 100–300 range and 12x appreciation cap

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (precision-math) · Phase 2 (math-precision, periphery agents)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.timeMultiplier (945-953)

timeMultiplier grows +50 per year indefinitely past year 3, despite NatSpec explicitly documenting a "100-300" range calibrated against the 50,000,000 divisor for a "1.0x-12.0x" appreciation range. At 13 years, timeMultiplier=700 (vs. the documented max 300); combined with H-1's self-reported maximal metrics, value reaches ~28x stake instead of the intended 12x, amplifying the yield-pool drain per bond-year of age. Overflow of the underlying multiply chain was traced by multiple agents and found practically unreachable for any realistic ETH-denominated stake — this is an economic/calibration bug, not an arithmetic-overflow bug.

Recommendation: Cap timeMultiplier at 300 to match the documented range and divisor calibration.

[L-2] createBond's "stake 30% of quarterly revenue" invariant is unenforceable

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (precision-math, general) · Phase 2 (invariant, first-principles agents)
Location: AIAccountabilityBondsV2.createBond (388-419)

require(msg.value >= (quarterlyRevenue * 30) / 100) is trivially satisfied when the caller-supplied quarterlyRevenue = 0, letting anyone open a bond with an arbitrarily small stake while claiming any "quarterly revenue" figure — the value is never externally validated and feeds no other logic besides this self-satisfiable check.

Recommendation: Require quarterlyRevenue > 0, or drop the on-chain "enforcement" framing since the figure is fundamentally unverifiable.

[L-3] Owner pause() has no time bound and no non-owner emergency exit

Origin: [phase1 only] (dos, access-control, governance) — re-examined against source and confirmed
Location: BaseDignityBond.pause/unpause (68-80) gating distributeBond/requestDistribution/createBond

Once paused, AI companies have no path to recover stake or appreciation except waiting for the owner to unpause. Notably withdrawYieldPool and resolveChallenge are not gated by whenNotPaused, so the owner retains fund-moving power while ordinary users are frozen — an asymmetric emergency-powers design.

Recommendation: Add a bounded maximum pause duration, or a permissionless emergency-withdraw-principal path that remains available while paused.

[L-4] Dead/decorative safety constants and state create a documentation-vs-code trust gap

Origin: [both] — Phase 1 (oracles, precision-math, general) · Phase 2 (invariant, asymmetry, numerical-gap, periphery agents — _replenishYieldPool specifically)
Location: MIN_AI_VERIFICATIONS, MIN_ORACLE_TRUST_SCORE (never read outside declaration); oracles/registeredOracles (write-only); lastDistributedValue (write-only, its stated anti-replay purpose is actually fulfilled only by the active flag); BaseYieldPoolBond._replenishYieldPool (documented "losses go back to pool," never called anywhere — the depreciation branch of distributeBond pays from general balance and never replenishes yieldPool)

Multiple state variables and constants exist purely as unused scaffolding implying protections (oracle trust gating, minimum peer verification, appreciation replay protection, "zero-sum: losses fund future gains" self-healing) that do not actually operate. This is the direct root cause of H-1.

Recommendation: Either wire these into the enforcement paths they were clearly designed for, or remove them to avoid misrepresenting the trust model to users and integrators.

[L-5] Unbounded view-function loops — off-chain/dashboard griefing surface only

Origin: [phase1 only] (dos, general) — re-examined against source and confirmed
Location: getProtocolOverview (1251-1268), getBondsByCompany (1278-1307)

Both are external view functions looping over 1..nextBondId. Confirmed (by tracing every internal caller) that no on-chain state-changing function calls either, so this cannot brick a transaction — it only degrades off-chain dashboard/eth_call consumers as bond count grows, which is cheap to inflate on Base.

Recommendation: No change required for on-chain safety; consider better pagination for dashboard consumers if nextBondId grows very large.

Informational

  • Divisor documentation mismatch: calculateBondValue's NatSpec comment (line 957) states the divisor is "100,000,000" while the actual code and "Math" block use 50,000,000 — align the comment.
  • Modifier ordering nitpick: resolveChallenge declares onlyOwner nonReentrant rather than nonReentrant onlyOwner; harmless here (no external call precedes the guard) but inconsistent with house style.
  • MissionEnforcement.acceptOwnership omits the explicit pendingOwner != address(0) check present in BaseDignityBond's otherwise-identical two-step transfer (unreachable in practice, since msg.sender == address(0) cannot occur, but inconsistent); it also lacks a cancelOwnershipTransfer counterpart.
  • yieldPool vs. real ETH balance can drift across the depreciation-payout branch, resolveChallenge's penalty payout, and accumulated (never-withdrawn) verification stakes — all three add/remove real ETH from address(this).balance without a matching adjustment to the yieldPool accounting variable. Individually these are covered by H-4/H-5/L-4 above; flagged here as a combined "the ledger and the vault can silently diverge" observation worth a dedicated reconciliation pass if the protocol continues development.
