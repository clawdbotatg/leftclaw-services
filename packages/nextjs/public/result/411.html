[MEDIUM] M-1. Incumbent delegated roles (feeRecipient / buybackExecutor / fallbackResolver) can indefinitely veto the owner's own attempt to replace them

[both — Phase 1: evm-audit-access-control AC-2; Phase 2: boundary + access-control agents, both with full concrete PoCs] · Confidence: 92

Location: TreasuryRouter.sol — cancelFeeRecipientRotation() (line 379-386), setFeeRecipientOverride/acceptFeeRecipientOverride (349-372), proposeFeeRecipient (318-325); cancelBuybackExecutorRotation() (175-182); ArbitrationModule.sol — cancelFallbackResolverRotation() (~163-173).

Description: The owner's dedicated recovery path for a compromised/rogue delegated role
writes into the same pending-slot the incumbent can also write and cancel. Confirmed at
source: proposeFeeRecipient (self-initiated, msg.sender == feeRecipient) and
setFeeRecipientOverride (onlyOwner) both assign pendingFeeRecipient (lines 321, 351);
cancelFeeRecipientRotation (line 379) accepts msg.sender == feeRecipient || owner() and
unconditionally deletes whichever proposal is pending, with no distinction between a
self-initiated and an owner-initiated (recovery) proposal. The identical shape exists for
buybackExecutor (cancelBuybackExecutorRotation, line 175) and for fallbackResolver.

Proof of concept: (1) feeRecipient key is compromised. (2) Owner calls
setFeeRecipientOverride(cleanAddr) at time T → pendingFeeRecipient = cleanAddr,
feeRecipientRotationProposedAt = T. (3) The compromised incumbent immediately calls
cancelFeeRecipientRotation() (passes, msg.sender == feeRecipient) → pendingFeeRecipient
deleted. (4) acceptFeeRecipientOverride() now reverts "Treasury: no pending recipient". (5)
Owner retries; incumbent cancels again; repeat indefinitely — there is no cooldown or nonce on
either side. Meanwhile the incumbent continues calling withdrawFees/withdrawNativeFees,
draining usdcReserve, all AZL accruedFees, and slashed native ETH. The identical shape lets a
rogue buybackExecutor keep 40% of protocol USDC revenue flowing to itself indefinitely, and a
rogue fallbackResolver remain seatable on disputes.

Impact: For feeRecipient specifically, this is an ongoing, unrecoverable drain of protocol
treasury reserves with no on-chain remedy short of a full contract redeploy. For the other two
roles, impact is bounded (buyback funds are protocol revenue, not user funds; fallback resolver
is constrained by escrow's party-match checks on split/refund).

Recommendation: Give the owner-initiated override path its own pending slot/flag
(pendingOverrideRecipient + overrideProposedAt) that proposeFeeRecipient/incumbent-cancel
cannot touch; restrict cancelFeeRecipientRotation/cancelBuybackExecutorRotation/
cancelFallbackResolverRotation to reject cancellation of an owner-initiated recovery proposal
from anyone but the owner.

[MEDIUM] M-2. Emergency arbitration-module recovery can be indefinitely blocked by a cheap, permissionless self-dispute griefing loop

[phase1: evm-audit-access-control AC-1] · Confidence: 80

Location: ArbitrationModule.sol — setDisputeOpeningPaused() (195-225; MAX_PAUSE_DURATION = 7 days at line 31, re-pause cooldown nextPauseAllowedAt = now + 14 days at line 221); ArbitrationRecoveryCoordinator.sol — _validateSafeOutgoing() (~175-190) gates executeRecovery on disputeOpeningPaused()==true, activeDisputeCount()==0, pendingSideEffectCount()==0.

Description: The highest-privilege action in the system — atomically swapping the arbitration
module across all 4 dependent contracts to recover from a compromised/buggy module — requires the
outgoing module to be simultaneously paused with zero active disputes. A pause auto-expires after
7 days and cannot be re-armed for 14 days (line 221), while disputes can legitimately stay open up
to ABSOLUTE_RESOLUTION_TIMEOUT (21 days). Any address can permissionlessly open a cheap
self-dispute (direct-hire task to a second controlled address, ~$1 bond, refundable via timeout)
during every forced-live 7-day window between pauses, keeping activeDisputeCount() > 0 across
every subsequent pause attempt — so "paused ∧ zero active disputes" is never simultaneously
reachable, permanently blocking recovery exactly when it is most needed.

Proof of concept: Owner pauses at T0 (paused window T0..T0+7d, re-pause blocked until
T0+14d). At T0+7d (pause expired) an attacker opens a dispute — its absoluteDeadline extends to
~T0+28d. Owner re-pauses at T0+14d (window to T0+21d) but the T0+7d dispute is still active. The
attacker repeats every re-opened window; proposeRecovery/executeRecovery always revert on
"Recovery: active disputes".

Recommendation: Decouple the recovery-safety gate from the auto-expiring, cooldown-limited
pause — e.g., a dedicated non-expiring "recovery lockdown" state, or allow recovery once every
remaining dispute is past its own absoluteDeadline (permissionlessly force-resolvable via
resolveTimedOut) rather than requiring activeDisputeCount()==0.

[MEDIUM] M-3. resolveStaleReview's cross-contract review-clock computation underflows (reverts) if the arbitration module is replaced while a task is IN_REVIEW

[both — Phase 1: evm-audit-general G-1 + evm-audit-dos DOS-1, independently found the identical bug] · Confidence: 85

Location: TaskRegistry.sol — snapshot at submitProof() line 510 (_reviewPausedTimeAt[taskId] = arbitration.totalDisputeOpeningPausedTime()); use at resolveStaleReview() lines 553-562 (... + (arbitration.totalDisputeOpeningPausedTime() - _reviewPausedTimeAt[taskId])).

Description: totalDisputeOpeningPausedTime() (confirmed monotonic non-decreasing within one
module instance, ArbitrationModule.sol:239-246) is snapshotted from whichever module arbitration
currently points to. If the module is later replaced via
ArbitrationRecoveryCoordinator.executeRecovery → emergencySetArbitration (the exact mechanism
M-2 discusses), the new module's counter starts at ~0. Any task that entered IN_REVIEW while the
old module had already accumulated pause time (snapshot S > 0) will, after the swap, compute
newModule.total() - S in Solidity 0.8's checked arithmetic — reverting whenever the new
module's accumulated pause time is less than S, which is true right after any swap. This
permanently bricks the permissionless optimistic-accept escape hatch (resolveStaleReview) for
every task IN_REVIEW at swap time.

Proof of concept: Owner pauses disputes on module A once, accumulating 5 days of pause time,
then unpauses. Worker submits a milestone proof → _reviewPausedTimeAt[taskId] = 5 days, task
state IN_REVIEW. Governance later executes an emergency recovery swap to fresh module B
(B.totalDisputeOpeningPausedTime() ≈ 0). Anyone calling resolveStaleReview(taskId) after the
3-day timeout computes 0 - 5 days → underflow → revert, permanently for that task (the
milestone is still recoverable via acceptMilestone, completeTask, or a real dispute — just not
via the fast permissionless path).

Recommendation: Clamp the subtraction: uint256 delta = cur >= snap ? cur - snap : 0; so the
function degrades gracefully (treats a post-swap module as contributing zero extra pause) rather
than reverting.

[MEDIUM] M-4. ReputationRegistry.stakeVerifierBond overwrites (shortens) an arbitration-imposed bond lock, letting a seated arbitrator dodge the ruling-slash

[phase2 — independently found by 7 of 12 attack agents: invariant, economic-security, asymmetry, and first-principles reported it as a full FINDING with matching numeric traces; boundary, access-control, and execution-trace reported it as a corroborating LEAD] · Confidence: 90

Location: ReputationRegistry.sol — stakeVerifierBond() line 231
(verifierBondUnlockAt[msg.sender] = block.timestamp + VERIFIER_BOND_COOLDOWN;, unconditional)
vs. lockVerifierBond() lines 252-253 (if (unlockAt > verifierBondUnlockAt[verifier]) { ... },
extend-only).

Description: When both parties mutually consent to an arbitrator, proposeArbitrator calls
lockVerifierBond(arbitrator, d.absoluteDeadline) specifically so the arbitrator cannot unstake
their slashable ETH bond before a post-ruling penalty applies. stakeVerifierBond writes the
same storage slot (verifierBondUnlockAt) but does so unconditionally, which can move the
unlock time earlier than an active arbitration lock — the exact inverse of lockVerifierBond's
carefully guarded extend-only semantics.

Proof of concept (converged on by 7 independent agents): Dispute opens day 0,
absoluteDeadline = day 21. Arbitrator seated day 1 via mutual consent →
lockVerifierBond(arb, day21) sets unlockAt = day21; evidenceDeadline = min(day1+7, day21) = day8.
At day 1 the arbitrator calls stakeVerifierBond{value: 1 wei}() → unlockAt resets to
day1+7 = day8 (13 days earlier than the lock). At day 8, in the same block: unstakeVerifierBond (fullBond) succeeds (now >= day8), then resolveDispute succeeds (now <= evidenceDeadline).
_prepareVerifierBondSlash reads verifierBond(arb) == 0, computes amount = 0, and returns
early — the documented 10% ruling-penalty (ReputationRegistry.sol comment at line ~249,
"prevent front-running a ruling slash by unstaking") is fully evaded.

Impact: No direct third-party fund theft — the loss is that the system's only per-ruling
economic penalty against a biased/self-dealing arbitrator can always be reduced to zero by a
rational actor. This compounds with the related fallback-resolver gap (Low-2 below) and the
arbitrator-reputation-farming lead (Low-3 below): a farmed, low-trust arbitrator faces no real
slash risk either.

Recommendation: Make the assignment monotonic, mirroring lockVerifierBond:

uint256 target = block.timestamp + VERIFIER_BOND_COOLDOWN;
if (target > verifierBondUnlockAt[msg.sender]) {
    verifierBondUnlockAt[msg.sender] = target;
}

[MEDIUM] M-5. EscrowVault._settleAtDeadline's MILESTONE branch pays out the worker's full self-declared (unverified) claim on arbitration timeout, with no ruling

[phase2 — numerical-gap and execution-trace agents, independently, both flagged as LEAD with matching mechanism] · Confidence: 62

Location: EscrowVault.sol — _settleAtDeadline() lines 306-352, MILESTONE branch at
line 342-343 (workerAmount = ITaskRegistry(taskRegistry).pendingMilestoneLiability(taskId);),
reached via the permissionless 21-day-timeout path resolveTimedOut → _finaliseTimeout → settleArbitrationTimeout → _settleAtDeadline.

Description: pendingMilestoneLiability is incremented purely from the worker posting an
arbitrary 32-byte hash via submitProof (TaskRegistry.sol:483-516) — nothing on-chain verifies
the underlying work. For STREAMING/HOUR_BLOCKS modes, the same timeout path pays only
objectively-verifiable, time-accrued amounts; for MILESTONE, it pays the worker's unverified claim
in full if the dispute simply runs to the 21-day absolute timeout without any arbitrator actually
ruling.

Proof of concept / bound: A worker submits a fraudulent or incomplete milestone proof, the
poster opens a dispute, and if 21 days pass with no ruling, resolveTimedOut pays the worker the
full claimed amount. This is meaningfully bounded — both agents who found it explicitly note that
assignFallbackResolver/replaceTimedOutArbitrator are permissionless and callable by the poster
(or anyone) from day 7 through day 21 to force a real neutral ruling instead of letting it time
out — so realizing this requires the disputing poster to take no action across the entire window
despite having an available remedy.

Recommendation: Consider defaulting the pure-timeout MILESTONE settlement toward a
poster-favoring or split outcome (rather than the worker's unverified claim in full) absent any
ruling, and/or add an explicit EscrowMode.UPFRONT arm to the same function (see Low-6 below,
same function, distinct mechanism) so all four modes have an intentional, documented timeout
default rather than three handled cases and one silent fallthrough.

[LOW] L-1. ArbitrationRecoveryCoordinator.executeRecovery has no upper-bound/expiry, unlike every other rotation mechanism in the system

[both — Phase 1: AC-3; Phase 2: boundary agent, same root cause] · Confidence: 75

Location: ArbitrationRecoveryCoordinator.sol — executeRecovery() line 126-145; contrast
with ArbitrationModule.executeFallbackResolverRotation (delay + ROTATION_GRACE_PERIOD
upper bound) and TreasuryRouter.acceptBuybackExecutor/acceptFeeRecipient (same pattern).
Confirmed at source: executeRecovery checks only block.timestamp >= recoveryExecutableAt
(line 129) with no corresponding <= upper bound.

Description: A forgotten/abandoned proposeRecovery remains permissionlessly executable
forever once its safety preconditions (outgoing module paused, zero active disputes, zero pending
side effects, consistent peer graph) happen to coincide again — e.g. the next time the owner
pauses disputes for routine maintenance. An owner-chosen-but-possibly-stale module could then be
installed unexpectedly.

Recommendation: Add a symmetric expiry, e.g.
require(block.timestamp <= recoveryExecutableAt + RECOVERY_GRACE_PERIOD, "Recovery: expired");

[LOW] L-2. Fallback-resolver seating never locks the resolver's verifier bond — the ruling-slash is unconditionally bypassable on that path

[phase2 — boundary, access-control, asymmetry, and periphery agents, independently] · Confidence: 70

Location: ArbitrationModule.sol — assignFallbackResolver and replaceTimedOutArbitrator
never call reputationRegistry.lockVerifierBond, unlike the consent-seat path in
proposeArbitrator (which does, at ~line 405). When a fallback resolver later rules via
resolveDispute, _finalise still runs with byArbitrator=true, so _prepareVerifierBondSlash
attempts a 10% slash against a bond that was never frozen — the resolver can unstake at any time
with no timing dependency at all (unlike M-4, this requires no window-alignment trick). This also
directly contradicts the code's own comment stating "Timeout and fallback paths do not slash."

Recommendation: Call lockVerifierBond(d.snapshotFallbackResolver, d.absoluteDeadline) inside
both functions, mirroring the consent-seat path.

[LOW] L-3. Arbitrator reputation/tier is farmable via self-dealt disputes once a single owner bootstrap grant exists

[both — Phase 1: evm-audit-general G-2; Phase 2: trust-gap agent produced a full concrete numeric PoC, promoting this from a lead to a confirmed mechanism; flow-gap corroborated] · Confidence: 78

Location: ArbitrationModule.sol — proposeArbitrator tier gate (~363-379), resolveDispute
(~441-457); ReputationRegistry.recordArbitratorResolution (fixed +50 reputation, no decay, no
independence check between dispute parties and arbitrator).

Description: An address controlling poster + worker + arbitrator wallets can self-deal a $1
tier-1 dispute (mutual consent is trivially satisfied when both parties are the same controller),
rule on it, and gain +50 reputation / +1 resolvedCount at near-zero net cost (the escrow and
forfeited dispute bond both return to the same controller). Five repetitions reach
MIN_REP_TIER2 (200) + MIN_RESOLUTIONS_TIER2 (5), qualifying for real, high-value tier-2
disputes. Requires one legitimate/owner-granted qualifyBootstrapArbitrator (or a genuine first
tier-1 seat) to bootstrap; escalation from there is fully self-service. Using the farmed
arbitrator against a real counterparty still requires that counterparty's independent mutual
consent, bounding the ultimate damage to reputation-signal integrity rather than unilateral theft.

Recommendation: Require a meaningfully-sized, slashable stake before reputation accrues from
a resolution, and/or rate-limit reputation gain per (arbitrator, counterparty-set) to make
self-dealing uneconomical.

[LOW] L-4. Verifier-bond slash side-effect can become permanently un-retryable, potentially blocking emergency recovery indefinitely

[both — Phase 1: evm-audit-general G-3; Phase 2: boundary, invariant, periphery, flow-gap, and first-principles agents all independently flagged the same mechanism, all explicitly noting they could not force a trigger] · Confidence: 45 (below the numbered-finding confidence floor in spirit, kept here given how many independent agents converged on the exact same mechanism)

Location: ArbitrationModule.sol — _trySlashVerifierBond/_prepareVerifierBondSlash
(~588-616); gate at ArbitrationRecoveryCoordinator._validateSafeOutgoing requiring
pendingSideEffectCount() == 0.

Description: The slash amount is snapshotted (bond/10) at finalise time. If that inline
attempt ever defers (try/catch) and the arbitrator's bond later drops below the snapshotted
amount (enabled by M-4/L-2, or simply after the lock lapses), every retry via retrySideEffects
reverts on verifierBond[subject] >= amount, and the pending flag can never clear — permanently
blocking executeRecovery, the system's highest-privilege action. All six agents who examined
this (across both phases) explicitly state the inline slash normally succeeds and they could not
construct a forced first-attempt deferral, so this is reported as a well-corroborated but
unconfirmed-trigger liveness risk.

Recommendation: Make the deferred slash saturating (min(amount, currentBond)) rather than
all-or-nothing, so a queued slash can always eventually clear.

[LOW] L-5. EscrowVault._settleAtDeadline has no branch for EscrowMode.UPFRONT — the worker gets nothing on any non-ruling terminal path

[phase2 — flow-gap agent] · Confidence: 65

Location: EscrowVault.sol — _settleAtDeadline() lines 314-343: if (STREAMING) {...} else if (HOUR_BLOCKS) {...} else if (MILESTONE) {...} — confirmed via source read that UPFRONT
(enum value 0) matches none of the three arms, leaving workerAmount = 0 and
posterAmount = remaining (100% refund to poster).

Description: Every other escrow mode pays the worker something on expiry/timeout
(time-accrued for streaming/hour-blocks, self-declared claim for milestone — see M-5); UPFRONT
alone pays the worker zero and refunds the poster in full on any expireTask/resolveTimedOut
path. A worker who delivered upfront work loses their entire payment if the poster simply stalls
past the deadline or a dispute times out — bounded by the worker's own ability to permissionlessly
call assignFallbackResolver to force a real ruling instead.

Recommendation: Add an explicit UPFRONT arm to _settleAtDeadline with an intentional,
documented default (e.g., pay the worker in full on expiry if the task was ACTIVE, since upfront
delivery is all-or-nothing) rather than silently falling through to zero.

[LOW] L-6. Whole-marketplace liveness depends on continuous AZL/USDC transferability; a paused/blocklisted token bricks fee-charging or vault-flush entrypoints (issuer-triggered, not attacker-triggerable)

[phase1: evm-audit-erc20 E20-1, E20-2, E20-3, consolidated] · Confidence: 70

Description: (a) If USDC's issuer blocklists either shared vault contract itself (EscrowVault
or AgentDepositVault), every outbound transfer reverts and pooled funds freeze with no rescue
function — inherent to custodying a centralized-issuer token, not a code defect. (b)
flushBuybackShare/flushStakerShare/flushTreasuryUsdc push to fixed, non-redirectable
addresses; if one of those (e.g. buybackExecutor) is blocklisted, that revenue bucket stalls
until a governance rotation completes (up to 4 days). (c) Every fee-bearing task action pulls AZL
via transferFrom; if AZL is paused (e.g. a pre-graduation lock), postTask/claimTask/
createTask/dismissWorker/leaveTask all revert — new-task creation and exit flows are DoS'd
until AZL is unlocked, though in-flight settlement is unaffected.

Recommendation: Document these as accepted centralized-token-custody dependencies; consider a
flushBuybackShareTo/similar variant so a blocklisted fixed destination doesn't require a full
rotation to unblock revenue.

[LOW] L-7. UnionStakingVault: rewards emitted during a zero-stake interval are captured entirely by the first re-opening staker; credit cap-crossing forfeits the final partial period rather than prorating

[both — Phase 1: STK-1, STK-2; Phase 2: trust-gap ("carried-reward capture") and numerical-gap/math-precision ("accumulator-vs-total-drift", "cap-forfeiture-griefing"), independently confirmed] · Confidence: 68

Description: (a) When totalStaked == 0, notified/carried USDC rewards park and re-stream in
full over a fresh 7-day window to whoever stakes first, even 1 wei — not stranded (conservation
holds) but "first re-opener take-all" rather than pro-rata; mitigated by the 7-day stream (not
instant capture) and dilution if others join. (b) When an accrual step would cross
CREDIT_CAP (600,000), that step issues zero credits and permanently closes issuance rather than
prorating to fill exactly to the cap — whoever triggers accrual first keeps their earned credits,
whoever waits loses the whole step. Both are edge-case/timing-dependent and, at realistic AZL
stake levels, the credit cap is not practically reachable (~1.8×10¹³ AZL·days required).

Recommendation: For (a), consider routing zero-stake-interval rewards back to treasury
reserve rather than the next staker, if that better matches intent. For (b), prorate the final
step to fill exactly to the cap (periodCredits = min(periodCredits, CREDIT_CAP - totalCreditsIssued)) before closing issuance.

[LOW] L-10. TreasuryRouter.distributeRevenue/flushStakerShare timing/liveness edges

[phase2: periphery ("griefing-crank-timing"), economic-security ("fund-stranding")] · Confidence: 50

[LOW] L-11. EscrowVault/AgentDepositVault _payOrDefer treats a nonstandard-but-successful return as failure, which would double-pay for a token with odd return-data length

[phase1: evm-audit-general G-4] · Confidence: 40

Recommendation: No action needed while USDC-only; flag if the vaults are ever pointed at a
different token.
